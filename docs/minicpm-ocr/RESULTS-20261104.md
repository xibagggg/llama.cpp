## v35b: compensation and physical N padding

Tested source: `2e231e33963b84326a2b853ada4d97d162abcc6b`. F32 activations split into FP16 high and residual parts; residuals are scaled by 4096 during packing. Two FP32 matrix products are combined after reduction. N tails pad physical storage to eight and bound logical stores. TCM scratch is 346624 bytes per worker.

The actual forward() microbenchmark tests N1/7/8/988/1008/1020/1060, with finite-output, canary, output-tail and independent sampled FP64 checks. Large-N speedups are 4.69–5.08x over RVV/M4; relative L2 is about 1.81e-6. Small N1/7/8 is slower due to preparation cost and must not be promoted as a general fast path.

The first unscaled-residual N1 test failed the preset relative-error threshold (9.04e-6 versus 2e-6). The scaled version passed the same threshold. This observation does not establish the hardware subnormal mode as the cause.

In seven full OCR quality cases, six have identical text and token IDs. The scanned-table candidate still changes a previously correct field into an incorrect field. One full warmup plus three formal repeats per flag state reproduce internally stable but different outputs. Table structure and numbers are preserved. The model-wide error source is not yet localized; synthetic operator error cannot stand in for OCR accuracy.

The seven full OCR timings are single quality requests, not formal speed ABBA. No v35b model-wide speed or quality promotion is claimed. Multi-slice embedding dumps cover the last slice only. Historical eight-worker drift is unresolved.

## v36-v40: numerical diagnosis, selected down layers and exact attention reuse

Tested source: `d84277ecaaa87cfbc22dcfb5d34109ae4bc37d8a`. All tests ran on 2026-11-03; the campaign heading is user-specified. Experimental flags remain off by default.

Actual OCR tensor replay located loss of tiny FP16 inputs in the previous IME2 path. Two isolated cases, 2^-24 times 1 and 1 times 2^-20, previously returned zero. Power-of-two weight scaling and residual handling restore their exact results. All 27 actual-input down replays pass the preset 2e-6 relative-L2 threshold, with FP64 sampling and output guards.

The normalized all-layer path still changes a scanned-table field. Layer-1 Q/K/V input relative L2 of 4.4118454e-7 changes 24,851 of 1,444,608 packed Q8 bytes, including scales; matrix-output differences then grow. This supports amplification at dynamic quantization thresholds, not an image-blur explanation.

The selected candidate keeps layers 0-13 on the original RVV/M4 path and enables normalized IME2 only for layers 14-26. D72 attention shares K/V across eight queries and pre-packs K/V once per attention call, cooperatively across workers. Each output element retains its original arithmetic order. Storage is transient, bounded by shape guards, and not cached across requests.

The attention microbenchmark covers N64/65/988/1008/1056, contiguous/strided layouts, masks and tails. Complete output bytes, finite outputs and guards pass in all 20 combinations, for both pack-only and pack-plus-eight-query variants. N around 1000 improves about 8-17% with both, including preparation cost; small-N gains are inconsistent.

Seven full OCR quality requests retain baseline text and complete token IDs. Attention changes retain the selected-down candidate embedding exactly. Selected-down versus original-path last-slice embedding still differs about 1.3-1.7%; seven cases do not establish general OCR equivalence. Existing OCR mistakes remain.
